Who this is built for

We work with fewer clients than most agencies because we go deeper. Before we look at options, be honest with yourself about whether this describes your situation.

This is for you if:

  • Early-stage or solo founder (or a very small team) making most of the calls yourself
  • You're already acting as your own CTO, CFO, or ops person, and it's starting to show
  • You have real decisions in front of you (what to build next, what you can actually afford, what to automate) but no one to pressure-test them with
  • You already use, or are willing to use, tools like Claude, Notion or ClickUp, Google Workspace, and spreadsheets for your financials
  • You want a system that learns your business and gets sharper over time, not a one-off prompt you'll forget
  • You're ready to hand off the repeatable parts of the job so your hours go to the decisions only you can make

This is not for you if:

  • You want someone to run the whole business for you with zero involvement from you
  • You're looking for a single clever prompt, not a system wired into your actual tools
  • You don't have, or don't want, accounts for the tools we'd connect your co-founder to
  • You're already big enough to hire a real CTO and CFO, and just haven't gotten to it yet
  • You're not willing to walk us through how you currently make decisions, even roughly, so we can build around it
  • You want this to replace talking to customers or making the calls only a founder can make (it won't, and shouldn't)

The solo-founder ceiling

You didn't plan to be the bottleneck in every function. Here's how the story usually goes for a founder running the business mostly alone:

  1. 1

    Founder Does Everything

    You wear every hat because you have to. At this size, it's actually the fast way to move: no handoffs, no waiting on anyone else's calendar.

  2. 2

    The Juggling Act

    The business grows, but you don't duplicate. Every bug, every pricing question, every recurring report still routes through you. Decisions start queuing up behind you instead of flowing through you.

  3. 3

    The Bottleneck

    You can't justify a $150K+ CTO or a $120K+ CFO yet, and you can't keep doing both well while also doing the one job only you can do: setting direction, talking to customers, closing the next deal. Growth stalls because you're the bottleneck in every function at once.

Does that sound familiar? The fix isn't working more hours, and it isn't doing every function yourself, forever. It's having a system that actually knows your business well enough to take real work off your plate.

What inaction is actually costing you

This is the part most founders don't calculate: not the cost of setting up an AI co-founder, but the cost of not having one. It's the cost you're already paying, quietly, every week you keep doing every job yourself.

No technical co-founder, so every product call and bug waits on you

When there's no one to turn ideas into shipped product, it either doesn't ship, or it ships slowly, through expensive contractors who don't know your business the way you do.

No CFO, so you're guessing at runway, pricing, and margins

Cash questions get answered with gut feel instead of a model you can actually trust, until a surprise shows up in the bank balance and there's no time left to react.

Repetitive ops work eats the hours you need for the big decisions

Reporting, scheduling, inbox triage, and status updates quietly consume the exact hours that should be going toward the decisions only a founder can make.

Hiring the team you actually need costs $250,000–$400,000+ a year, or your equity

A CTO and CFO combined routinely run six figures each before either is proven, and bringing on a real co-founder instead means splitting ownership of the business you built.

Here's what makes it worse: most founders know this isn't sustainable, but keep pushing through anyway, because hiring feels premature and doing it all yourself feels like the only option. It isn't, once the system exists.

FAQ

Is this actually Claude, or your own AI tool?

It's Claude, the real thing, inside your own Claude account. We configure it, Projects, Claude Code, and MCP connections, to work as your co-founder. You're not locked into a tool only we control.

Can it actually write and ship code?

Yes, through Claude Code connected to your repository. It can write features, fix bugs, and open pull requests for your review. How much you let it do autonomously versus just review is up to you.

Will it replace a financial advisor or accountant?

No. It builds and maintains the financial models and reporting most early-stage founders don't have time to build themselves, and flags things worth a closer look. For tax filing, audits, and formal compliance, that still goes through a licensed accountant. We can connect you with one through our partner network if you need it.

What if my business changes, do I have to rebuild the whole thing?

No. Part of the build is monthly tuning, so the system evolves with your business instead of going stale. A bigger pivot might need a deeper rework, but the core workspace and connections carry over.

What's the minimum commitment?

The Co-Founder Build runs for an initial 60 days, the time it takes to wire the system properly and see it running on real decisions. After that, continue month-to-month for tuning and support, or take the system and run it yourself.

Do I need to already be technical to use this?

No. Most of our solo-founder clients aren't. The whole point is that Claude handles the technical and financial depth and explains it back to you in plain language, so you don't need a CS or finance degree to make the call.

What tools does this actually connect to?

It depends on your stack, but commonly: your codebase through Claude Code, Google Workspace for email and docs, Notion or ClickUp for your project tracker, and your spreadsheets or accounting software for financials. We map the exact list during the Blueprint phase.

I'm not a founder, I'm a busy executive. Does this still apply to me?

Yes. The same setup works for an executive running a function or a P&L mostly alone: an AI workspace wired into your real tools that handles the recurring technical, financial, and reporting work, so your time goes to the decisions only you can make.
