The single-post ceiling

You've got a real audience worth growing. The content just hasn't caught up yet. Here's how the story usually goes for a founder-led account:

  1. 1

    The Early Days

    You post when you remember. A single image here, a reshared story there. It feels like enough because you're busy running the actual business.

  2. 2

    The Plateau

    Reach stalls. Instagram's algorithm deprioritizes single-image posts, so even decent content barely gets seen. You post more often, but it's still images and stories — the format was never the real problem, the system was.

  3. 3

    The Ceiling

    Whoever's filming — usually the founder — can't keep up personally forever. Follower growth flatlines. The account goes quiet during exactly the weeks the business needs it most: a launch, a hire, a client crunch.

Does that sound familiar? Posting more isn't the fix. Posting the right formats, consistently, through a system that doesn't depend on any one person's week being free — that's the fix.

What inaction is actually costing you

This is the part most founders don't calculate. Not the cost of building a content system — the cost of not having one. The cost you're already paying, silently, every week you keep posting without a plan.

Single-image posts get buried by the algorithm, so the account stops growing

Instagram now prioritizes carousels and reels over static images. Every image-only post is reach left on the table — and every week without carousels or reels is a week of audience growth you don't get back.

You're the one posting the reels, so content stops the moment you get busy

When growth content depends on the founder personally filming and posting, it never survives a busy week. The account goes dark at the worst possible time — precisely when the business needs visibility most.

Copy, visuals, and video don't match, so even good content undersells the idea

A strong hook paired with a generic stock-style AI image reads as off-brand and gets scrolled past. Conceptual mismatch between what's said and what's shown is one of the most common — and most avoidable — reasons content underperforms.

Hiring a full-time social media manager costs more than you'd think, before results show up

An experienced in-house social media manager or content creator typically runs $48,000–$72,000+ a year, before design and AI tool subscriptions, plus a ramp period to learn your brand voice. And if they leave, you start again.

Here's what makes it worse: most founders know single images aren't working, but keep posting them anyway because building an actual system — pillars, templates, a daily rhythm — feels like a bigger project than reposting a story. It isn't, once the system exists.

FAQ

How long until we see results?

Templates and the first system-produced content ship within the first 2 weeks. A consistent daily cadence is typically running by week 3–4. Audience growth compounds over months, not days — content is a long game, and we optimize weekly based on real reach and engagement data.

Do you guarantee results?

We guarantee the system: content pillars defined, templates built, daily cadence running, and iterated weekly based on performance. We do not promise a specific follower count or a viral post, because that depends on variables outside the system itself — your market, existing audience size, and how distinct your offer is.

What is the minimum commitment?

The System Build runs for an initial 60 days. We ask for 60 days because it takes roughly 30 to build and launch the system properly and 30 to optimize based on real performance — cancelling before that means we haven't had time to make it work. After 60 days, continue month-to-month or cancel with 30 days notice.

We've tried posting consistently before and it didn't move the needle.

This is the most common thing we hear. “Consistent posting” that doesn't work is usually still single images and reshared stories — just more of them. What you had was a habit, not a system. Carousels and reels with locked copy, a daily minimum per account, and weekly iteration based on real data is a different approach entirely. Apply and tell us what you've tried; we'll tell you exactly what we'd do differently.

Do we own the templates and workflows at the end?

Yes. Everything is built in your own AI tool accounts — Claude Design, OpenArt, HeyGen — and organized in your Google Drive. We configure and run it, but you own all of it. If you ever stop working with us, you keep the full system.

Do I have to be on camera for all the video content?

No. We build an AI avatar trained on your likeness using HeyGen, so you can produce on-brand reels and video content without filming yourself for every single piece. You can still film when you want to — the system just doesn't depend on it.

Do I have to publish everything myself?

No. We set up a Make automation scenario connected to your ClickUp or Notion content calendar. Content queues up on schedule, and you choose whether each post goes out for your review first or publishes automatically — your call, per account.

How is this different from hiring a social media manager or a content agency?

A social media manager or agency usually means a monthly retainer with no defined system — a handful of posts, a generic calendar, and a monthly report. We build an actual production system in your own accounts: content pillars, templates with copy locked before design, AI avatar and image production instead of stock photos, automated scheduling, and weekly performance reviews. You end up owning the system either way — theirs disappears when you cancel, ours doesn't.
